Galena's Historic Square and Its Newer Neighborhoods

Galena was established in 1809 at the confluence of the Big and Little Walnut Creeks, and its village square still includes genuine 1820s construction alongside the 1906 Galena Vault building. New subdivisions began being built starting in 2005, which means Galena, like Sunbury, genuinely has two distinct housing eras rather than one uniform profile, and a home's plumbing risk depends heavily on which era it falls into.

A home near the square is a real candidate for original galvanized supply lines and an older sewer connection, while a post-2005 subdivision home has modern PEX or copper and a different, more recent set of likely issues.

Living at the Headwaters of Hoover Reservoir

Galena sits right at the point where the Walnut Creeks feed into Hoover Reservoir, and part of the original village was demolished when the reservoir was built in 1953, which tells you how close this town genuinely sits to real water. Homes near the creek confluence and the reservoir's edge see real groundwater pressure against foundations, particularly during spring thaw and after heavy rain, making basement waterproofing and sump pump reliability a genuinely higher-stakes concern here than in a town farther from water.

A Small Village With Real Growth

Galena's population grew from 924 residents according to the 2020 census to an estimated 1,018 by 2023, a real and continuing growth pattern that mirrors nearby Sunbury on a smaller scale. That growth means more new-construction plumbing calls alongside the historic square's older-home repair work, and we treat each on its own real merits rather than applying one standard approach to the whole village.

What We Check First on a Galena Call

Given how much of Galena's real risk profile ties to water, from the reservoir to the creek confluence, a sump pump and basement moisture check is a standard part of most visits in this town, even when the original call was about something else entirely.
