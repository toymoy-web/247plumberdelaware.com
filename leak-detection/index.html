Water Bill Climbing But You Can't Find a Leak? Here's What's Happening

If your water meter is still moving with every fixture in the house shut off, water is going somewhere you can't see. That's most often a slab leak, a leak inside a wall, or a failing underground line between the meter and the house. "Leak detection" is the process of finding that exact location electronically, using acoustic and thermal equipment, rather than opening walls or the slab on a guess.

This matters because the alternative, cutting into drywall or concrete at a guessed location, is expensive and often wrong. A confirmed location from real equipment means the repair itself, whether that's a small access cut or a larger job, targets the actual problem the first time.

What Electronic Leak Detection Actually Finds

Acoustic listening equipment picks up the specific sound water makes escaping a pressurized line, which travels through pipe and surrounding material differently depending on depth and material type. Thermal imaging can reveal a temperature difference where water is saturating a wall or slab, especially useful for a hot water line leak where the temperature contrast against surrounding material is more pronounced.

Combined with pressure testing, which isolates sections of plumbing to narrow down which zone of the house actually has the leak, most leaks are located within a small, confirmed area before any cutting begins.

Signs Worth Getting Checked Before They Get Worse

A warm spot on an otherwise cool floor, especially over a slab, often means a hot water line leak underneath. The sound of running water when every fixture in the house is off is one of the clearest signs of a hidden leak anywhere in the system. A water bill that jumps significantly with no change in household water use is worth investigating even without any visible sign, since a slow underground leak between the meter and the house can run for weeks before it surfaces.

Checking Your Own Meter Before You Call

Locate your water meter, usually near the street or in a basement utility area, and make sure every fixture and appliance that uses water is off, including ice makers and irrigation systems. Watch the small leak-indicator dial, often a red or black triangle, for a minute or two. If it's moving at all with everything off, water is escaping somewhere in the system, and that observation alone is useful information to share with us when you call. Leak detection and any resulting repair work fall under our Delaware Public Health District plumbing registration, per the state standard set by Ohio House Bill 487.
