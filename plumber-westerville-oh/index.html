Westerville Sits on a County Line, and Your Plumbing Paperwork Does Too

Westerville is a city of about 39,000 people split between Franklin and Delaware counties, and its ZIP codes reflect that split. ZIP 43081 is mostly in Franklin County, while ZIP 43082 sits on the Delaware County side, in the area around Genoa and Harlem townships. We serve both sides from our Delaware base.

The split matters more than it sounds. A house near Hoover Reservoir and a house a few streets into Franklin County can both carry a Westerville mailing address and still answer to different local offices. We confirm which county and municipality your address belongs to before any permit is pulled, so a water heater replacement or a repipe does not stall on paperwork after the work is done.

Uptown Westerville and the Streets Around Otterbein University

The older core of Westerville centers on the Uptown Westerville Historic District and Otterbein University, which has anchored the city since the 1840s. Houses in and around Uptown include homes built well before copper and PEX became standard, so original galvanized supply pipe and cast iron drain lines still turn up behind walls and under floors.

Mature street trees add a second factor. Roots work their way into aging clay and cast iron sewer laterals at the joints, and the first sign is usually a slow drain that keeps coming back after being snaked. A camera inspection tells us whether a lateral needs a spot repair or something larger before anyone digs up a front yard.

Newer Homes Between Hoover Reservoir and Alum Creek

On the Delaware County side, Westerville addresses reach toward Hoover Reservoir and Alum Creek, with Genoa Township sitting between the two. ZIP 43082 is a premium housing market, with a median home value near $433,000 in recent census estimates, and much of it was built in the last few decades.

Those homes generate a different set of calls. Larger houses with several bathrooms strain a single water heater, finished basements make a quiet sump pump failure expensive, and builder-grade fixtures are reaching the age where supply stops, fill valves, and faucets fail within months of each other. Central Ohio's hard water adds scale to every one of those parts.

Groundwater, Sump Pumps, and the Alum Creek Side of Town

Alum Creek and Hoover Reservoir shape the local water table. Homes closer to either one see more groundwater pressure during spring thaw and after long rains, and a basement that stayed dry for years can start seeping once the soil around the foundation settles.

We test a sump pump by filling the pit rather than trusting the float, and we confirm the discharge line empties well away from the foundation. In a finished basement, a battery backup is worth pricing before storm season instead of after the first flood.

Getting Across the County Line Quickly

Westerville is the southern edge of our regular dispatch zone, roughly sixteen miles from downtown Delaware by road. Our dispatcher sends the crew nearest your side of town and routes by whatever is moving best that hour, whether that means US 23, State Route 3, or Interstate 71.

The Genoa Trail, part of the Ohio to Erie Trail, links Westerville with Galena, and that corridor is one reason we treat the two towns as neighbors when we schedule routes. A call from Westerville does not wait for a crew to cross the whole county.

Emergency Dispatch Around the Clock in ZIPs 43081 and 43082

Our emergency line covers every Westerville address in ZIP 43081 and ZIP 43082 at any hour, from Uptown to the reservoir shoreline. A burst pipe on a holiday morning gets the same response as a water heater failure on a Tuesday afternoon.

Beyond Westerville itself, we also serve the neighboring 43035, 43065, and 43021 ZIP codes, and we pair that reach with the sump pump and water heater work that Westerville homes call about most.
