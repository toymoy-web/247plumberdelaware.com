What Landlords Near Ohio Wesleyan Should Know About Galvanized Pipe

Written by the Delaware 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated October 12, 2026

Call(513) 796-9381

Ohio Wesleyan University's roughly 1,300 students create real, ongoing demand for off-campus rentals in the older neighborhoods around downtown Delaware. A lot of that rental housing was built well before modern copper and PEX plumbing became standard, which means galvanized supply pipe is still genuinely common in this specific segment of the local housing stock.

Why This Matters More for Landlords Than Owner-Occupants

A homeowner living in a house with aging galvanized pipe notices gradual symptoms firsthand, like slowly declining water pressure or the occasional rust-colored tap water. A tenant often doesn't have that context, and a plumbing symptom that a longtime owner would recognize as a slow, known issue can come to a landlord instead as an urgent complaint or even a habitability concern.

Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out over decades, narrowing the pipe's interior diameter and eventually causing exactly that kind of rust-tinted water, especially noticeable after water sits in the pipes overnight. It's a real, physical process, not a plumbing scare tactic, and it's worth understanding before a tenant's complaint turns into an emergency call.

What to Check Before You Call a Plumber Between Tenants

The gap between one tenant moving out and the next moving in is the easiest time to have a plumbing system genuinely assessed, since the property is empty and any needed work doesn't disrupt anyone living there. Checking water pressure at multiple fixtures, running the water and checking for discoloration after it's sat overnight, and having a plumber look at accessible supply lines all take relatively little time during a turnover.

A recurring pinhole leak in more than one location, rather than a single isolated repair, is the strongest signal that the property's plumbing is nearing the point where a full repipe makes more financial sense than continuing to patch individual failures as they come up.

Repair or Repipe: The Real Trade-off

A single, isolated pipe issue in an otherwise sound system is usually still worth a targeted repair rather than a full repipe. Once failures start recurring in different parts of the same property, though, the math tends to shift.

Repeated emergency calls, each with their own service fee and potential water damage exposure, often cost more over a few years than one planned repipe would have cost upfront, on top of the tenant relations cost of repeat plumbing emergencies in an occupied unit.

Getting a second, honest opinion before committing to either path is reasonable, especially for a larger, older property where the cost difference between a repair and a full repipe is significant. A plumber who explains the actual condition found, not just a recommendation, gives you enough information to make that call confidently as the property owner.

Documentation That Protects You Later

Keeping a simple record of every plumbing service call at a rental property, including what was found and what was repaired, builds a real paper trail that matters if a tenant dispute or an insurance question ever comes up.

A pattern of recurring pipe failures documented over time also makes it easier to justify a repipe's cost to a co-owner or lender if the property is jointly held or financed. The case is built on real service history rather than a single plumber's opinion.

For a multi-unit or multi-property portfolio, a standing relationship with one plumbing company that already knows your properties' plumbing age and history is generally more efficient than starting from scratch with a different company for every call, since real context carries over between visits.

What to Tell a Tenant Who Reports Discolored Water

A tenant reporting rust-colored water understandably wants reassurance, and the honest answer depends on what's actually causing it. A brief discoloration after water has sat unused for a day or two, which clears after running the tap for a minute, is a different situation than water that stays discolored no matter how long it runs.

The first is a common, if inconvenient, symptom of aging galvanized pipe. The second warrants a same-week inspection rather than a wait-and-see response, since it suggests more advanced internal corrosion.

Budgeting for an Aging Rental Property's Plumbing

Setting aside a real, dedicated maintenance reserve for an older property's plumbing, rather than treating every repair as an unplanned expense, changes how these decisions get made. A landlord who's already budgeted for the likelihood of a repipe within the next several years can make that call proactively, during a vacancy, on their own timeline.

A landlord caught off guard by a third emergency call in a year is making the same decision reactively, usually at a worse price and a worse time.

What to Ask a Plumber Before Hiring for a Rental Property

Ask whether they'll provide written documentation of what was found and repaired, since that record matters for future decisions and any dispute that might arise later. Ask how they handle tenant coordination and access, since an occupied rental has different scheduling needs than a vacant home. A plumbing company that already understands rental property dynamics, rather than treating every call the same as a routine homeowner visit, makes ongoing property management noticeably smoother.

Delaware, Ohio Local Note: Inspections and Lead Testing

Delaware Public Health District inspects new plumbing in residential buildings in Delaware County, and noncompliant work can result in a red-tag inspection. Ask any plumber who works on your rental whether the job needs a district permit.

The City of Delaware's 2022 water quality report says lead in drinking water comes primarily from service lines and home plumbing. The city reported 0 of 31 sampled sites above the lead action level in 2021, but a single building can differ, so test before you assume.

Common Questions From Delaware Property Owners

Am I responsible for pipe age I didn't cause?

As a property owner, yes, ongoing maintenance responsibility typically falls on you regardless of when the original plumbing was installed. Understanding your property's actual pipe material and age helps you plan for that responsibility rather than being surprised by it.

Should I disclose known galvanized pipe to a prospective tenant?

Disclosure requirements vary by lease and local practice, but being upfront about a known issue, along with your maintenance plan for it, tends to build more trust than staying silent and hoping it doesn't come up during the tenancy.

How do I know if my specific property has galvanized pipe?

A visible section in a basement or utility area often shows a dull gray color with visible threading at joints, unlike copper's reddish tone or PEX's flexible plastic appearance. A plumber can confirm definitively during any service visit if you're unsure.

Does a repipe increase a rental property's value?

A documented repipe is a genuine, disclosable improvement that removes a known future liability, which matters to both prospective buyers and prospective tenants evaluating a property. It's a real capital improvement, not just a maintenance expense, in most practical terms.

Is it worth repiping only part of a property instead of the whole thing?

Sometimes, if the failures are genuinely concentrated in one area, like a single bathroom or kitchen, rather than spread throughout the property. A real assessment of where problems have actually occurred, rather than a blanket assumption either way, is what determines whether a partial or full repipe makes more sense for that specific building.

Who inspects plumbing work at a rental property in Delaware County?

Delaware Public Health District inspects new plumbing in residential and commercial buildings in Delaware County. Noncompliant work can result in a red-tag inspection, which delays the job. Rentals in Dublin and Westerville are outside the district's plumbing program.
