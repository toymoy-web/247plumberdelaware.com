Why Boiler Heat Is Still Common in Older Delaware Homes

Homes built in Delaware before forced-air furnaces became standard, particularly in the older neighborhoods near downtown and Ohio Wesleyan University, often still run their original boiler-based radiator or radiant heat systems, sometimes updated over the decades but fundamentally the same design. These systems can last far longer than a typical furnace when properly maintained, which is part of why so many are still in service.

Common Boiler Problems We Diagnose

No heat at all, with the boiler not firing, often points to a thermostat, ignition, or pressure switch issue rather than anything catastrophic. Banging or knocking noises, sometimes called kettling, usually mean mineral buildup on the heat exchanger, a real issue given Delaware's water hardness in a boiler system that uses standing water repeatedly. A boiler that's losing pressure gradually points to a small leak somewhere in the system, which we trace before simply refilling and hoping it holds.

Repair or Replace an Aging Boiler

A boiler under 15 to 20 years old with a specific, diagnosable fault is usually worth repairing, since boilers generally have a longer service life than furnaces when maintained. A boiler well past that age with a cracked heat exchanger, or one requiring frequent repairs each season, reaches a point where replacement becomes the more sensible long-term decision, which we discuss honestly based on the specific unit's condition.

Annual Maintenance Worth Doing

A yearly inspection catches small issues, like a worn circulator pump or a pressure switch drifting out of calibration, before they cause a no-heat call in the middle of a cold snap. Flushing the system periodically also helps manage the mineral buildup that Delaware's hard water contributes to over each heating season, which reduces the kettling noise and efficiency loss that buildup eventually causes.

Radiator and Zone Valve Issues

One cold radiator while others heat normally usually means trapped air in that specific line or a stuck zone valve rather than a problem with the boiler itself. Bleeding the radiator is a simple homeowner fix worth trying first, though a zone valve that's stuck open or closed needs a technician to diagnose and replace correctly.
