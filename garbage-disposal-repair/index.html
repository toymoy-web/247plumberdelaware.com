Humming, Jammed, or Silent: Three Different Problems

A disposal that hums but the blades don't spin almost always means something is physically jammed inside, and a reset button press after clearing the jam usually restores function without needing a new unit. Common culprits include a stray fruit pit, a piece of glass, or a utensil that fell in unnoticed. A disposal that's completely silent, with no hum at all, more often points to a tripped reset button, a bad switch, or in some cases a wiring issue, which is a different diagnosis path entirely.

Leaking underneath the sink, rather than a jam or silence, usually comes from a worn seal where the disposal connects to the sink flange or the dishwasher line, and it's a common failure point once a unit passes 8 to 10 years of regular use. Left unaddressed, that kind of leak can damage the cabinet floor underneath before it becomes obvious from a quick glance.

Repair or Replace

A jam, a tripped reset, or a leaking connection are all repairable on most units regardless of age. A motor that's burned out, or a unit that's leaking from the housing itself rather than a connection point, is not economical to repair and is the point where replacement makes more sense.

What to Check Before You Call

Make sure the disposal is actually getting power. A tripped reset button on the unit's bottom or a tripped breaker are both common and simple to rule out before assuming a bigger repair is needed. Never put a hand into the disposal, even when it's off, since a stuck object can be cleared safely with tongs or the Allen wrench method built into most units.

If the disposal is leaking, check whether the water is coming from above the unit at the sink flange, from the dishwasher hose connection on the side, or from the bottom of the housing itself. Each location points to a different repair, and it helps us bring the right part on the first visit.
