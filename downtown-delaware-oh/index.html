About Historic Downtown Delaware

Downtown Delaware grew around Ohio Wesleyan University and the county courthouse in the late 1800s and early 1900s. Sandusky Street is its commercial heart, a three block stretch of shops, restaurants and offices, and the Sandusky Street corridor is the designated National Register historic district.

Downtown is not only commercial. As the area revived, apartments were added on the second floors above storefronts, so one building can hold a business at street level and one or more homes above it.

Plumbing in Buildings That Mix Businesses and Homes

When a restaurant, a shop and an apartment share one building, they often share parts of the plumbing too. A clog at street level can show up as a slow drain upstairs, and a leak above can damage the business below. Finding the true source comes before deciding who pays for what.

We start by asking who uses which fixtures and where the water was first noticed. Then we trace the problem with the right tool, such as a drain camera for a stubborn clog or leak detection when water appears with no visible source.

Restaurants, Cafes and Drain Care

Food service puts heavy demands on drains. Grease, food scraps and constant use can slow a kitchen line quickly. Hydro jetting clears buildup from the pipe wall, and regular drain cleaning costs far less than an emergency call in the middle of a dinner rush.

Water Heaters for Small Commercial Spaces

A small shop or office has different needs than a house. A tank sized for a few hand sinks is not sized for a kitchen or a busy salon. We talk through capacity and location, and we check whether the current unit can be fixed through water heater repair before suggesting a replacement.

Working Inside a Historic District

The City of Delaware created its Downtown Historic District in the 1990s. Any exterior alteration inside it needs a Certificate of Appropriateness from the Historic Preservation Commission, in addition to normal building or zoning permits.

Most plumbing happens inside the walls. If a job would change the outside of a building, ask the city planning office whether approval applies before work starts. We describe what the job involves so you can have that conversation.

Burst Pipes and Overflows in Shared Buildings

Water in a building with neighbors above or below cannot wait. Close the shutoff for the affected fixture or the building main if you can reach it, move valuables, and call us. See our burst pipe repair page for what to do in the first minutes.
