When Does a Whole-Home Repipe Actually Make Sense?

"Repiping" means replacing a home's supply lines throughout, usually with modern copper or PEX, rather than patching individual sections as they fail. It makes sense specifically when a home's plumbing is old enough, usually original galvanized steel from before the 1960s, that failures are recurring across multiple locations rather than isolated to one bad section.

It does not make sense for a home with one or two isolated pipe issues in otherwise sound plumbing, where a targeted repair addresses the actual problem without the cost and disruption of a full repipe. We evaluate the whole system honestly before recommending either path.

Signs Your Delaware Home May Need a Repipe

Galvanized pipe, common in Delaware homes built before the 1960s, corrodes from the inside out over decades, narrowing the pipe's interior diameter and eventually causing rust-colored water, especially noticeable after the water sits unused overnight. Recurring pinhole leaks in different locations, rather than one section failing once, point to system-wide corrosion rather than a single bad spot. Noticeably low water pressure throughout the house, not just at one fixture, is another sign the pipe's interior has narrowed significantly with buildup.

Copper vs. PEX for a Repipe

Copper has a long track record and handles heat well, making it a reliable, traditional choice, though it costs more and takes longer to install than PEX. PEX is flexible, faster to install since it needs fewer joints, and typically costs less, though some homeowners prefer copper's longer history and resale-value familiarity. We install either based on what fits your home and budget, without pushing a specific material by default.

Minimizing Disruption During the Project

A repipe means accessing walls and ceilings throughout the home, but we plan the work in stages so most of the house keeps some water access rather than everyone going without water for the entire project. Drywall repair after access cuts is typically not included in the plumbing quote, so we discuss upfront whether you're handling patching yourself or want that coordinated as part of the overall project.

What Happens to Fixtures During a Repipe

Most repipes connect into your existing fixtures rather than requiring their replacement, so your faucets, toilets, and shower valves generally stay as they are unless you're planning to update them separately. Older fixtures with worn shutoff valves are a good candidate to replace at the same time, since the access is already open and doing it separately later means paying for that access twice.

Registration for Repiping Work

A whole-home repipe, like any significant plumbing work in Delaware, follows the state registration standard set by Ohio House Bill 487, administered through the local plumbing inspection authority.
