About the Historic Northwest District

The Historic Northwest District in Delaware was added to the National Register of Historic Places on February 29, 1996. The listing counts 510 contributing buildings, meaning the buildings that add to the historic character of the district.

The district is roughly bounded by Pennsylvania Avenue, North Sandusky Street, West William Street, Elizabeth Street, West Fountain Street and North Franklin Street. If you are not sure whether your street counts, give us the address when you call.

Plumbing in an Older Neighborhood

Age alone does not tell us what is inside a wall. Houses in a historic district have usually been renovated several times, so the plumbing may be original, partly replaced or entirely modern. We look at what is really there before we recommend a repair.

That is why a visit starts with questions. Where did you notice the problem, how long has it been going on, and has any work been done in that part of the house before?

How We Check an Older Home

A sewer camera inspection shows the condition of the line from the house to the street, including cracks, sags and roots. Leak detection finds hidden water without opening walls on a guess. We also check shutoff valves and fixtures, since a valve that will not turn is a problem in an emergency.

You can help by finding your main water shutoff and turning it fully closed and open once a year. Look for stains on ceilings and walls, and note any slow drains.

Renovations and Owners Who Preserve

Many owners in the district are restoring or updating a house rather than replacing it. Work that opens walls, adds a bathroom or moves a kitchen is easiest to plan alongside the rest of the renovation, so talk to us before the drywall goes back up. Our shower and bathtub installation page covers bathroom work.

National Register listing recognizes a district for its history. Whether a specific exterior change needs local review is a question for the City of Delaware planning office, so ask them before you change the outside of a historic house.

Water Heaters, Basements and Emergencies

In any older house the water heater and the sewer line are the two parts people forget until something fails. A tank leaking at the base usually means the tank itself has failed, and our water heater repair page explains the options. If a basement takes on water, shut off power to wet areas first and then call.

For a burst pipe or active leak, close the main valve, clear the area and call (513) 796-9381. The line is answered 24/7.
