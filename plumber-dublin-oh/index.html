Dublin Is Mostly a Post-1970 Housing Market

In 1970 Dublin was a village of 681 people. Today it is a city of about 49,000, and the growth came largely from communities like Muirfield Village built around the golf course. That timeline means most Dublin plumbing is fifty years old or younger, with copper and later PEX supply lines rather than the galvanized steel found near downtown Delaware.

Younger pipe does not mean trouble-free plumbing. Homes from the 1980s and 1990s are now at the age where original tank water heaters have been replaced once or twice, toilet and faucet internals wear out together, and finished basements show their first sump pump failures.

One City, Parts of Three Counties

Dublin sits in Franklin, Delaware, and Union counties, with the bulk of the city in Franklin County. ZIP 43016 covers the western and central part of the city, and ZIP 43017 covers the eastern and northern part. Those are the only two ZIP codes Dublin uses.

We serve the whole city from Delaware, and the portion nearest our home county is the shortest drive. Before we pull a permit for a water heater swap or a repipe, we confirm which county and municipality your address falls under, because that decides who inspects the work. Our work follows the state plumbing licensing standard set by Ohio House Bill 487, and we confirm which local health district handles registration for your county.

Historic Dublin and the Bridge Street District Are Opposite Plumbing Cases

Historic Dublin is the site of John Sells' original purchase on the west bank of the Scioto River, and its older storefronts and converted buildings hold the city's oldest plumbing. Shared drain lines, aging cast iron, and restaurant kitchens that need regular grease line cleaning are the typical findings in buildings of that age.

The Bridge Street District is the other end of the spectrum, a 1,100-acre redevelopment along the river with new apartments, condominiums, retail, and offices. Multi-unit and mixed-use buildings call for commercial-style work, from backflow testing to banks of water heaters, and we handle that alongside residential service.

Muirfield Village, Tartan Fields, and Larger Homes

Neighborhoods such as Muirfield Village, home to the Jack Nicklaus-designed course that hosts the Memorial Tournament, and Tartan Fields are known for large, well-built houses. More bathrooms mean bigger water heaters and longer pipe runs, which is where tankless units and recirculation lines earn their cost.

Bigger houses also magnify small leaks. A pinhole in a supply line behind a finished wall can run for weeks in a home with this much plumbing before a water bill or a stain gives it away. Acoustic and thermal leak detection finds it without opening walls at random.

Ravines and Sloped Lots Along the Scioto

The Scioto River and its tributaries cut ravines through Dublin, and some lots sit well above or below the street. Where a house sits below street grade, sewer backups and sump discharge become real concerns, because wastewater and storm water have to climb to reach the main.

We check whether a basement has a sewer backflow valve, where the sump discharges, and whether the yard grade sends rain toward the foundation. Those checks take minutes and prevent the calls that come in at two in the morning during a storm.
