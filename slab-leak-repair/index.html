What a Slab Leak Actually Is

A slab leak is a leak in a water line running underneath your home's concrete foundation, usually the hot water supply line, though cold lines can leak too. Because the pipe is embedded in or under the slab, the leak doesn't show up as a puddle right away. Instead it shows up as a warm patch on the floor, a sound of running water with fixtures off, or in worse cases, water actually coming up through a crack in the slab itself.

"Slab leak repair" covers a range of actual fixes, from a targeted access cut directly above the confirmed leak point, to rerouting the line entirely above the slab to avoid disturbing the foundation at all. Which one makes sense depends on the leak's exact location and how much of the surrounding line shows similar age-related wear.

Why Precise Location Matters More Here Than Almost Anywhere Else

Cutting into a slab without a confirmed location is both expensive and disruptive, and a wrong guess means paying for concrete repair twice. We use acoustic and thermal equipment to narrow the leak to a specific, confirmed spot before recommending any concrete work, the same core process used in general leak detection but focused specifically on under-slab plumbing.

Older homes in Delaware's downtown area, with copper supply lines original to construction, are the most common candidates for a slab leak simply due to age. Copper pipe embedded in concrete corrodes from the outside in over decades of contact with the surrounding soil and moisture.

Repair Options: Access Cut vs. Reroute

A single, confirmed leak point in an otherwise sound line usually gets a direct access cut, repairing just that section of pipe through the smallest possible opening in the slab. When the surrounding pipe shows the same age and corrosion pattern as the leak itself, rerouting the line entirely through the attic or an interior wall is often the more durable choice instead. That approach avoids leaving a line that's likely to leak again in a different spot next year.

What to Watch For While You Wait

If the slab leak involves a hot water line, you may notice your water heater running more than usual to keep up with water that's continuously escaping and being replaced. Turning off the water heater's dedicated shutoff valve, if you have one and can safely access it, limits ongoing water loss and heating costs until we arrive, though your home's main shutoff always works as a fallback if you're unsure which line is affected. Slab leak repair, like all our plumbing work, is performed under our registration with the Delaware Public Health District, per the state standard set by Ohio House Bill 487.
