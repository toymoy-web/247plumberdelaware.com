Sizing a New Water Heater Correctly

A tank sized too small runs out of hot water during normal use, especially in a household with multiple bathrooms running back to back in the morning. One sized too large wastes energy keeping water hot that never gets used. We size based on your household's actual bathroom count and peak usage pattern, not a single default size sold regardless of the home.

Delaware's real water hardness also factors into which unit makes sense. A tank with a larger sacrificial anode rod, or a tankless unit rated for harder water, both hold up better here than a standard budget model built for softer municipal water elsewhere.

Tank vs. Tankless for a Delaware Home

A standard tank costs less upfront and installs faster, which matters if your old tank has already failed and you need hot water back quickly. A tankless unit costs more to install but delivers continuous hot water and lasts longer, often 15 to 20 years versus 10 to 12 for a tank, though it needs more frequent descaling given Delaware's hardness.

For a straight one-to-one tank replacement with no other changes, installation is usually completed the same day. Switching from tank to tankless sometimes requires new venting or a gas line upgrade, which we confirm and quote honestly before starting.

What's Included in a Professional Installation

A correct installation goes beyond just connecting the new unit. We set the temperature and pressure relief valve to code, connect a proper expansion tank where required, and verify the gas or electrical connection matches the new unit's actual specifications rather than assuming the old connection is automatically adequate. Haul-away and disposal of the old tank is included, so you're not left figuring out how to move a heavy, water-filled unit yourself.

Timing Your Replacement Before It Fails

A water heater showing early warning signs, like rust-colored hot water or a small amount of moisture at the base, is worth replacing proactively rather than waiting for a full failure that floods a basement or utility closet. Replacing on your own schedule also means comparing options calmly rather than making a rushed decision the same day an old tank finally gives out. We're happy to do a no-obligation assessment of your current unit's condition even before it's an emergency.
