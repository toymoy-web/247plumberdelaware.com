About Glenross

Glenross is a large single-family subdivision on the southeast side of the City of Delaware, with development extending south of Cheshire Road. When fully built it is planned for 487 single-family lots on about 210 acres, and local reporting calls it the largest subdivision in Delaware governed by a single homeowners association.

Homes are built in sections, and the city approved several new sections between 2019 and 2021, with Pulte Homes building the remaining ones. Houses were therefore built at different times depending on the section, and we treat each home on its own facts.

Home Size and Hot Water Demand

The development standards call for lots of at least 8,450 square feet and homes of at least 1,600 square feet for one story or 1,800 for two stories. Larger family homes tend to use hot water at several fixtures at once, so water heater capacity and recovery matter.

Tell us how many people live in the house and how the hot water is used. We can talk through repair first and size a replacement if the tank has failed. See water heater replacement and water heater repair.

Softeners, Filters and Fixtures

In newer homes the supply pipe is rarely the first thing people notice. Appliances and fixtures are, such as a faucet that drips, a disposal that jams or a water heater that needs service. We handle faucet and sink repair and garbage disposal repair in the same visit when it makes sense.

The City of Delaware's 2022 water quality report lists hardness at 121 ppm, so many homeowners ask about a water softener. If your water comes from the City, a test at your own tap shows what you actually have before you buy anything.

Sump Pumps and Discharge

Many newer subdivisions use sump pumps to move groundwater away from the foundation. If your home has one, test it before storm season by pouring water into the pit and watching it start. See sump pump installation and repair for backup options.

Where a sump pump may discharge is a local question. Ask the City of Delaware Public Utilities Department before routing a new discharge line.

HOA Approval and Builder Warranties

Because Glenross is governed by one homeowners association, exterior projects such as a discharge line, a hose bib or a softener drain may need approval first. Check your association rules before work begins, and we will describe exactly what a job involves so you can submit it.

If your house is still under a builder warranty, call the builder about covered items before paying for a repair. For a burst line or active leak, stop the damage first and call (513) 796-9381. The line is answered 24/7.
